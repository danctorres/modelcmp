# modelcmp keys

Every key and mouse action in the TUI. Press `?` in the TUI for the short list. The
[README](README.md) covers install and the basics, [REFERENCE.md](REFERENCE.md) the columns,
commands and JSON.

Movement follows vim, and the arrow keys, `PgUp`/`PgDn` and the mouse work as well. In `?`,
`enter` on a key changes it.

## General

| Key | Action |
|-----|--------|
| `?` | help: every key and what it does, with a cursor on one. `/` finds a key, and `n` `N` go to the next / previous match. `enter` on a key of one letter or sign waits for you to press its new key, and `esc` cancels. As in a game's settings menu, the action that had the new key takes the old one. An action moved to a free key leaves its old key doing nothing. A key takes everything it does with it, in the lists too: moving `T` also moves editing a task in `f`'s grid. The tabs, the hints and `?` show the keys you set, with the default after each one you changed. `space`, `enter`, `esc`, `tab`, the arrows and the digits can't be changed and are grey. `enter` on `↺` puts every key back, once a second `enter` confirms it. The hint `enter change key` shows on a row whose key can change. Keys are saved in `user.json`. A hand edit there that gives two actions one key, or gives a key to no action, loses only that entry, and modelcmp says so at start |
| `esc` | back, one step per press: closes an overlay, drops the highlight, clears the `/` search, leaves `S`, `F` or `E`, and then goes from a task picked in recommend back to recommend |
| `qq` `^c` | quit. The first `q` asks, `^c` does not. It works from an open list too, unless you are typing in it, and any other key after the first `q` goes back to the list |
| `r` | refresh data now |
| `!` | lists what the last refresh warned about, as a harness installed but not logged in, one issue per line. While there are issues, the frame's bottom right says `⚠ refresh had issues`. `!` or `esc` closes the list. While the refresh's issues are there, the table's status bar has the hint `! issues` |
| `UU` | upgrade modelcmp when the frame's bottom left says a newer version is available. The first `U` asks. It closes the TUI, runs `brew upgrade` on a Homebrew install or `cargo install` of the new release on a cargo one, and starts the new version. For a binary installed by hand it opens the release's page instead |

## Move

| Key | Action |
|-----|--------|
| `j` `k` | move the cursor: a faint grey fill between two bars `▌` `▐` in the accent colour, on which the row keeps its colours. It looks the same in dropdowns and lists, on a model's row in compare, and on a task's name or tier in recommend. A count repeats the move, as in `3j`. From the last row `j` goes back to the first. The arrow keys work as `h` `j` `k` `l`, `PgDn` `PgUp` move a page, and `Home` `End` go to the top / bottom |
| `h` `l` | move to the column on the left / right. Model stays in view and the columns right of it scroll, Dev being the first to go. A count repeats, as in `3l`, stopping at the last column, and from there `l` goes back to the first. `‹` `›` mark columns off screen, and `▲` `▼` on the left border mark rows above or below. In compare they move along the columns too, with the same marks. In recommend they pick a tier of the task, `free` `low` `mid` `high`, and those keys and `space` act on its model. The task's name comes before its tiers. On the name, or on a tier with no model, those keys say there is no model there |
| `0` `_` `$` `w` `b` | `0` or `_` goes to the first column and `$` to the last. `w` `b` go to the next / previous group of columns: names and release date, prices and context, scores, speed (what a run measured), and Via and Notes. In compare `0` `_` `$` go to the first / last column too, and in recommend `0` `_` `$` pick the task's name / its `high` tier |
| `gg` `G` `3gg` | top / bottom / row 3. A number before `gg` goes to that row, as `12gg` to row 12, in dropdowns and lists too |
| `(` `)` `^u` `^d` | half a page up / down, stopping at the first / last row as `PgUp` `PgDn` and the wheel do. Overlays and dropdowns mark lines off screen with the same `▲` `▼` |
| `]` `[` | move the cursor to the next / previous selected model, wrapping from the last to the first. A count repeats, as in `2]`. In `f`'s grid and the dropdowns they go to the next / previous ticked entry |
| `}` `{` | move the cursor to the next / previous model you have access to, skipping the greyed `not available` rows that `a` shows. They wrap as `]` `[` do |
| `v` | highlight a range of rows: move to extend it, then `space`, `e` or `C` act on all of it. `C` compares just the range, and the models in it become the selected ones, in place of those selected before. `v` again or `esc` cancels |

## Filter and sort

| Key | Action |
|-----|--------|
| `s` | sort by the column, and again to reverse. In compare it sorts the models by the column. On Via it sorts by the harnesses, then by download size. When `↓ downloadable` is the only entry picked in Via's dropdown (`d`), it sorts by size alone and the top border says `by Via size`. Sizes not known yet are asked of Hugging Face, a screenful at a time |
| `/` | filter by name, developer, Via or note. Words can come in any order (`anthropic opus`), and a typo is forgiven when nothing matches (`opsu`). `/` again starts a new search and `enter` keeps what is typed. `esc` while typing goes back to the search before it, and in a panel or a list to where the cursor was too, and `esc` after `enter` clears the search.<br><br>In a panel (compare, the help, a model's details or recommend) every line stays. Matches are underlined, the cursor goes to the first, and `n` `N` go to the next / previous. Compare matches its rows by their labels, and recommend matches task names and the models in its tiers. A list like the theme panel works the same way: the cursor goes to the first entry that matches (in `f`'s grid, a task by its name), `enter` keeps the search and `n` `N` go to the next / previous match. `space` or `enter` then picks the entry under the cursor, or does nothing when nothing matches. Matches are underlined in yellow everywhere, and on a selected row's solid fill they get a yellow background instead |
| `>` `<` | set a minimum / maximum for the column, as `>` `60` `enter` on Coding. Ctx is in thousands of tokens, `>` `200` for 200k. Released takes a date: `>` `2026-06` is June 2026 on, and `<` `2026-06` up to the end of June. Via takes the size of the model's download in GB, `<` `8` for 8 GB at most. That hides models of unknown size and asks Hugging Face for every size not known yet, and typing it again asks again for those that did not answer. A maximum on Price replaces the level picked in its dropdown.<br><br>Models outside a bound are hidden, and `E` shows them. A bound is kept across restarts until `c` or `XX`, so `recommend`, `pick` and `--task` skip those models too, unless `--min` or `--max` on the same column replaces it. A bound on Via lasts only until the TUI closes. On a task column showing a benchmark picked in its dropdown, the bound is on that benchmark, in the commands too. On a column hidden with `\|`, a bound hides nothing until the column is back.<br><br>In recommend, `<` on a `low` or `mid` tier writes its price cap on the tier's header, as `enter` or `space` on the header or a click on it does (see `R`). The status bar shows `< price cap` on those tiers. `enter` applies the cap, an empty one puts the default back, and `esc` leaves it as it was |
| `n` `N` | go to the next / previous match of a `/` search in a panel or a list, wrapping around at either end as in vim. In a panel the status bar says which line of how many, and in recommend which match. `↓` `↑` do the same while you type the search, in a list too. In the table a search leaves only the matching rows |
| `d` | open the dropdown of a header marked ▾: Dev, Price, the task scores and Via. In recommend it opens the dropdown of the tier under the cursor, as every tier has a ▾ (see `R`). `/` searches it, as in every list. `space` or `enter` toggles an entry, and several can be on, as `space` selects several models. The dropdown stays open until `esc`. An entry not picked has a grey `✓`, as a model not selected does.<br><br>On Dev, Price and Via each entry also has a grey `✗` after it, as a table row does. `e` or a click on the `✗` leaves the entry out and turns the `✗` red, and doing it again takes the entry back. Leaving out a developer or a country hides its models, leaving out a price level hides the models at or under it, and leaving out a harness hides the models that only left-out harnesses have and that `x` cannot download. What you leave out is kept across restarts until `c`, `XX` or `e` on it again, and picking `all` keeps it. What you pick is kept until `c`, `XX` or a pick of `all`. Both hide models: picks hide the models of the entries not picked. Recommendations (`R`, `recommend`, `pick` and `--task`) skip the hidden models too, and `E` shows them. On the command line `--dev`, `--via` and `--max price` replace the picks. On Dev and Via `v` highlights a range of entries, as in the table: move to extend it, then `space` or `enter` picks them all, and `e` leaves them all out, or takes them all back when they all are. `v` again or `esc` cancels. A header's ▾ is yellow while an entry of its dropdown is picked or left out.<br><br>Dev lists countries first, as `China` or `USA`, when Epoch AI knows where the developers are from. A country shows every model of a developer from there.<br><br>On Price only one level applies at a time, and an entry picks it or drops it.<br><br>On ECI, Coding and Agentic an entry picks another benchmark, as `FrontierSWE`. ECI lists general benchmarks that still tell the best models apart, as `FrontierMath T4`. The column then shows that benchmark's score, 0-100, blank for a model not tested on it, and `s` sorts by it and `>` `<` bound it. The header keeps its name, marked `*` with its ▾ yellow, and the top border names the benchmark while the cursor is on the column. Each entry names its source, as `APEX-Agents · Epoch AI`. The first entry is the column's own score: `ECI`, `FrontierCode` or Arena's `Agent`. Picking it, picking the picked one again, or `c` goes back to the task's score. The list says how many models each benchmark scored. Recommend ranks the task by the picked benchmark too, showing its scores and saying `ranked by` it under the grid, and so do compare (`C`), with the column marked `*`, `recommend`, `pick`, `--task` and `compare`. The pick is kept across restarts until `c` or the first entry.<br><br>Arena's entries are with their task: its `Chat` rating in ECI's, its `WebDev` rating in Coding's, both from people's votes, and first in Agentic's the signals its agent score is made of: `Task outcome`, `Tool hallucination`, `Steerability`, `Bash reliability` and `Praise`. The column then shows that rating, or the net improvement on the signal in percent.<br><br>Via lists the harnesses, `ollama` and `llama-cli` for the models your machine runs, and then `↓ downloadable`: the models ollama or llama.cpp can download from their Hugging Face repo (`x`, `download and run`), most of them only in `all`. Leaving out `ollama` or `llama-cli` hides the models only they have. Leaving out `↓ downloadable` hides every model `x` can download, whoever else has it, and keeps the ones you cannot download. Leaving out `ollama` or `llama-cli` also keeps the models on your machine off recommend's tiers, and leaving out `↓ downloadable` the downloads on `all` and `✓ selected`, which they would otherwise take as free |
| `a` `m` | `a` is the `all` tab: every model, including the ones you have no access to, greyed out with `not available` under Via. `m` is the `mine` tab: the models you have access to, plus the selected ones you don't, which its `+2` counts. Either one leaves `S` `F` `E`. With access to no model, every model is shown and a line above the first says so |
| `B` | find a benchmark without knowing its task: a list of every benchmark of ECI, Coding and Agentic, each named with its source and its column, as `Remote Labor Index · Epoch AI · Agentic`, with how many models it scored. The ones the columns show have a `✓`, as in the dropdown. `/` searches it and `]` `[` go to the next / previous ticked one. `space` or `enter` picks the benchmark in its column, as that column's dropdown (`d`) does, moves the cursor there and closes the list. `esc` or `B` closes it without a pick. The list opens on the benchmark the column under the cursor shows, and picking the one a column already shows puts its task's score back. A column hidden with `\|` is not listed, and while a task is in use the list does not open |
| `\|` | the columns to show: a list of every column right of Model, each with what it means and a `✓` while it shows. `space` or `enter` toggles one, and the list stays open until `esc` or `\|`. `/` searches it and `]` `[` go to the next / previous ticked one. Saved in `user.json`. The command line prints every column, whatever you pick here |
| `tab` `shift+tab` | next / previous tab of the line above the table, `mine m   all a   ✓ selected S   ★ favorites F   ✗ excluded E   recommend R   compare C   theme t   help ?`, each with its key. They are the models you have access to, all models, only the selected, favorite or excluded ones (as `S` `F` `E`, one at a time), the recommend and compare panels, the theme list and the help. A tab, or a click on another one, leaves a panel or a model's details for it. Compare is greyed and skipped with fewer than 2 models selected, and any tab with nothing to show is greyed and skipped. The tab that is on is bold, inside a frame of its own whose sides run down into the table's frame, open at the bottom like a browser tab. A panel's tab sits closed on top of the frame, as the panel is a box of its own. A thin line separates two tabs that are off. In a narrow terminal, a tab that is cut off shows as a hint in the status bar instead. That holds in a panel too, where only the panels' keys work. A click on `mine` or `all` shows it, and a click on `✓` `★` `✗` turns it on or off as `S` `F` `E` do. Search, bounds and dropdowns still narrow the tab shown |
| `%` | Price with no input cached, as for a one-off prompt, instead of the share it started with (an agent's 90%, or `--cache`). Press it again to go back to that share. The top border on Price says which one is in use. The hint shows on the price columns, or anywhere while no input is cached. The cheapest provider and recommend follow it. `modelcmp --cache 50` starts the TUI with any other share |
| `c` | clear the filters, bounds, task, `S`, `F`, `E` and the dropdowns' entries picked and left out. The selected models and the ones excluded with `e` stay. It works in an open dropdown too, which stays open |

## Selected ✓, favorite ★, excluded ✗

Selected models (`✓`) are your shortlist, kept until you deselect them, across restarts too. A favorite
(`★`) is your pick for a tier of a task. Recommend shows it on that task's line as long as you have it and have not excluded it. An excluded model
(`✗`) is one you have but cannot use, so recommendations skip it.

| Key | Action |
|-----|--------|
| `space` | select the model, adding it to the shortlist you are deciding between. It stays selected until you deselect it, across restarts too. Its grey `✓` turns light blue, its name turns bold, and its row gets a faint light-blue fill on which it keeps its colours. A click on the `✓` toggles it. With the `terminal` theme the fill is mixed from the terminal's background, which modelcmp asks the terminal for at start. Some terminals don't report it, or don't say they draw 24-bit colour (`COLORTERM` is `truecolor` or `24bit`, `TERM` names kitty, alacritty, ghostty, foot, wezterm or a `-direct` terminal, or it runs under WSL. Over `ssh`, which drops `COLORTERM`, set it on the remote). There the fill is solid with the row black on it, the cursor is a reverse-video bar without the row's colours, and, as grey would not show, a model not selected has a `☐` and one not excluded a `·` |
| `f` | favorite the model for tiers of tasks (`coding:low`), which `--tier` then picks. Every row has a `☆`, which is a filled bold `★` for a favorite. With a task picked, the `★` marks that task's favorite in the task's colour, the colour of its name in recommend. With no task picked, a gold `★` marks a favorite of any task.<br><br>`f`, or a click on the `☆`, opens a grid with a row per task and a box per tier, `free` `low` `mid` `high`. A box has `✓` where the model is the favorite, `●` where another model is, and `☐` where there is none. It has `×` on a tier the model costs more than, which can't take it. The `×` is bold where the model is the favorite all the same, left there by a cap lowered after it, and `space` clears it. Each row ends with what the task is about, and the line under the grid says what the box under the cursor holds. `j` `k` move between tasks and `h` `l` along a row's boxes, a count repeating. `h` `l` skip the `×` boxes except a bold one. When `j` `k` reach a `×` box, the cursor goes to the nearest open box before it, else after it. A click on a `×` box says why it can't take the model. `space`, `enter` or a click ticks the box, and a click on a task's name goes to its row. A tick that makes the model a favorite opens `H`'s list for it, as a tier's dropdown in recommend does, and a pick or `esc` there goes back to the grid. The grid stays open until `esc`. `]` `[` go to the next / previous task with a tick, wrapping from the last to the first, a count repeating.<br><br>The grid starts on the picked task, or in recommend on the task under the cursor. In recommend it starts on the box of the cursor's tier, so `f` `enter` is for that tier alone. When the model costs more than that tier, as when it is a download that took the tier on `all` or `✓ selected`, the grid says why and starts on the next box that can take it. Elsewhere it starts on the box the model has for the task, else on the first box that can take it. With no task picked it starts on the first box the model has.<br><br>Your own tasks follow the built-in ones. The last entry, `+ new task`, takes a new task, its name and what it is about typed on the entry as with `T`, while the grid stays open (`Tool Dispatch` is saved as `tool-dispatch`). What it is about can stay empty. The new task gets the model on `high`, which takes any price. A name a task already has, with nothing written about it, goes to that task's row and ticks nothing. A task of your own has tier boxes as a built-in task does (`tool-dispatch:low`). Its `★` and name get a colour no built-in task has, picked by its name. It is gone once nothing in it is ticked and it has no about.<br><br>On one of your own tasks, `T` edits its name and what it is about together on its row (see `T`). The row shows the about, recommend shows it as when to use the task, and agents pick the task by it, over a built-in task that fits too. On one with nothing ticked and no about, `T` says why it can't.<br><br>`H` lists the model's harnesses, as on a row, and a pick or `esc` goes back to the box, where the line under the grid shows the harness as `via claude`.<br><br>On a selected row, `f` is for that row alone. A tier has one favorite, so with several models highlighted `f` says it takes only one, and a click on a `☆` is always for its own row. The status bar names the tasks the model under the cursor is the favorite for. `--tier` picks the favorite even when it is off the price frontier, and recommend shows it greyed there, marked `not recommended` under the grid |
| `T` | edit a task you added, in recommend (`R`) or in `f`'s grid. Its name and what it is about are written together on one line, starting from what they are, the cursor on the name: in recommend under the grid, where they show, the row showing the name as it is typed too, and in `f`'s grid on the task's row. `enter` in the name goes on to what it is about once the name can be, and `enter` there saves both. `tab` or `shift+tab` goes from one to the other, and `esc` drops both. A name another task already has, or no name, is said under it with the cursor in the name, and so is a task an agent renamed or deleted meanwhile. While a task is written in recommend, the lines under the grid are about it alone. A task with no model keeps its description, as it is all the task has, and `D` in recommend deletes the task. On a built-in task `T` says it can't, and elsewhere it says where it works |
| `e` | exclude the model: you have it but cannot use it. It stays in the table, greyed out like a model you have no access to, but recommendations (`R`, `recommend`, `pick` and `--task`) skip it. A greyed row keeps only the colours of its `✓`, `★` and `✗`, and the best and worst values are coloured among the other rows. Compare greys its column the same way. Every row has a grey `✗`, red when excluded. `e` or a click on it toggles the exclusion of that row alone, even on a selected one |
| `V` | highlight the selected models that are shown, as `v` highlights a range. Then `e` excludes them all, or unexcludes them when all are excluded, and `esc` cancels. The status bar hints it while a model is selected |
| `u` | deselect every model. The status bar hints it while a model is selected |
| `DD` | unfavorite every model, for every tier. Your own tasks lose their models too, and the ones with no about are gone. The first `D` asks, and any other key cancels. The status bar hints it in `F`. In recommend, `D` deletes a task of your own instead (see `R`) |
| `XX` | unexclude every model and show the ones the filters hide. It takes back the entries left out and picked in the Dev, Price and Via dropdowns, and the bounds. The first `X` asks, and any other key cancels. The status bar hints it in `E`, and outside `E` while the filters hide models |
| `S` | selected models only. `S` again or `esc`: every model |
| `F` | favorite models only: the rows with a `★` (with a task picked, that task's favorites), including the ones you have no access to, from `mine` as from `all`. `F` again or `esc`: every model |
| `E` | excluded models only: the rows with a red `✗`, including the ones you have no access to, from `mine` as from `all`. It also shows the models the filters hide, with a grey `✗`: those hidden by an entry left out or picked in the Dev, Price or Via dropdown, or by a bound. The status bar names the filter that hides the model under the cursor, as `hidden: OpenAI left out in Dev`. `E` again or `esc`: every model |

## Model under the cursor

| Key | Action |
|-----|--------|
| `i` | note for the model, written in its Notes cell, or on the `note:` line in details. Compare and recommend show no note, so there it is written in the status bar, as it is when `\|` leaves Notes out or the table is too narrow for it. `esc` and `enter` put the cursor back on the column it was on. `pick` and `recommend --json` show it to agents, who use it to choose between models |
| `y` `Y` | copy the model name / the model id. They work on a row, in details, compare and recommend, and in every list opened on a model: `o`'s, `H`'s, `f`'s grid and a tier's dropdown (`d`), on the model under the cursor there. In `x`'s list `y` copies the command under the cursor, a download's as you would type it, and `Y` the id its harness takes. The id is the one the model's harness (`H`) takes, else your default harness's, when it has the model. Otherwise it is the one its developer's own harness takes, as `claude-opus-5-5` for claude, else `provider/model` as opencode takes it, or as pi or omp take it when only they have the model. For a model only your machine runs, it is the tag `ollama run` takes, or the repo (`-hf`) or file (`-m`) `llama-cli` takes |
| `o` | open the model's page on models.dev, epoch.ai, artificialanalysis.ai, openrouter.ai or huggingface.co, whichever have one, and on arena.ai's agent leaderboard when it has an Arena score. It lists the sites to pick from, even when only one has a page, and `space` or `enter` opens one |
| `x` | open a harness on the model in a new terminal (Windows Terminal under WSL, else `$TERMINAL`, else `x-terminal-emulator`). It lists the harnesses that have the model, even when only one does, starting on the one `--cmd` starts (`H`). On a tier in recommend that is one whose own price fits the tier, as with `--tier`, and `Y` copies the id it takes. `space` or `enter` on a harness asks which folder to open it in. You type the folder on the harness's entry with the list still open, starting from the folder modelcmp runs in, so `x` `enter` `enter` opens it right here.<br><br>`tab` completes the folder's name as a shell does. When one folder starts with what you typed it completes to that folder, else to the first of them, and the lines under the list name them all, with the one in the path in bold. On a path ending in `/` they are every folder in it. `tab` again goes to the next folder and `shift-tab` to the one before. `enter` takes the folder in the path, so the next `tab` completes inside it. `~` is your home folder. `enter` opens the harness in the folder, and a path that is not a folder is said under the list. The word keys go by one folder of the path at a time, and `esc` leaves the entry for the list.<br><br>For a model with a repo on Hugging Face that ollama or llama.cpp does not have here, the list ends with `download and run`, which is `modelcmp get`, followed by the size of the download, as `(2.5 GB)`. When you have neither runner, the entry is `install, download and run`. modelcmp asks Hugging Face for the size once the cursor rests, for the model under it and the local ones on screen, and keeps it for the next runs. For a model Hugging Face has no GGUF copy of, the entry says so, and after that the model has no such entry until a refresh (`r`) or a week later. A model's details show that size too, also for one a runner here already has, as the size of its GGUF copy on Hugging Face. A model `x` can download has a `↓` under Via, in its details and in compare, followed by the size once known. The `↓` replaces `not available` when a download is the only way to get the model |
| `H` | the harness the model runs on, in Via dark on its colour. It lists `any harness`, then the harnesses that have the model, as `x` does, with the cursor on the one it has. `space` or `enter` picks one and `esc` leaves it as it was. It works on a row, in details, compare, recommend and `f`'s grid, which a pick or `esc` goes back to. `x`'s list starts on it, `Y` copies the id it takes, and `--cmd`, `--id` and `pick` start the model on it. With none chosen they use your default harness (`P`), else the model's developer's own, as claude for a Claude model, codex for OpenAI's and gemini for Google's, else the harness running modelcmp, as an agent's `pick`, else the first in Via's order. These last three skip a harness you left out in the Via dropdown while another has the model. `any harness` names the one that is, as `any harness (default: codex)`, in recommend among the harnesses whose price fits the tier. Saved in `user.json`, the same setting `modelcmp harness claude opus-5.5` sets |
| `P` | your default harness, for a model with no harness of its own (`H`). It lists `any harness (the developer's own first)`, then the harnesses you have a model on and did not leave out in Via, with the cursor on yours. `space` or `enter` picks one and `esc` or `P` leaves it as it was. The first start with two harnesses or more asks for it once, as the table opens, and says under the list that `P` changes it later. Saved in `user.json`, the same setting `modelcmp harness opencode` sets |

## Panels

| Key | Action |
|-----|--------|
| `enter` | details: every benchmark, price per provider. `space` `f` `e` `i` `o` `x` `H` `y` act on the model there too |
| `C` | compare 2 or more selected models: a verdict, then a row per model and a column per price, index and task. The verdict says which is cheapest, and for each task the best model, then each cheaper one that scores less, leaving out a dearer model that is no better. Your favorite for the task has a ★. A model with no value in a column has a `-` there, and a task or Tok/s is left out only when no selected model has a value for it. `j` `k` pick a model for `o` `x` `H` `y` `f` `e` `i`, and the table's cursor follows it. `h` `l` move along the columns and `s` sorts the models by one, as in the table. `enter` shows its details, and `esc` there goes back to compare. `C` again or `esc` closes compare |
| `R` | recommend: a grid with a row per task and a column per tier, `free` `low` `mid` `high`, each holding the model `--tier` picks, as `name · $price · score`. That is your favorite for the tier, marked `★`, when it costs no more than the tier's cap. Otherwise it is the best model at the tier's price: free for `free`, up to $2 for `low`, up to $5 for `mid` and any price for `high`. The header shows these caps, as `low ≤$2`. A model your machine runs takes a tier when it scores higher, shown as free. On `mine` a model you have not downloaded never does. On `all`, and on `✓ selected` when you selected it, one that fits in your memory (`modelcmp memory`) takes a tier too, with `↓` and its size, as `x` has to download it first.<br><br>The tiers' header is a row of its own between the `from` line and the first task, and `k` from the first task goes to it. There `h` `l` pick a tier, and the line under the grid says what that tier picks. `enter` or `space` writes the cap of `low` or `mid` on the header, the cap `modelcmp tier` sets. `enter` again applies it, an empty cap puts the default back, and `esc` leaves it as it was. `low` can't cost more than `mid`. Each task's line splits at these caps too, so a tier picks the best model up to its cap.<br><br>Each tier is as wide as its widest entry when the terminal has room. An entry wider than its tier wraps onto more lines inside it, with the price and score on a line of their own where they fit on one, so every name shows in full. A tier with no model shows `-`, and `enter` and the model keys say it has none. Under the grid, kept in view while the rows scroll in a terminal too short for them all, are the task under the cursor and when to use it on one line (for a task of your own, the description you wrote), with `ranked by` where its own score does not rank it, as `ranked by overall` for vision, and the model of the tier the cursor is on, in full, with `via claude` for a model given a harness (`H`). That line says `not recommended` for a favorite off the price frontier. For a favorite that is not the tier's own pick, a `recommended:` line names the model the tier picks without it.<br><br>The first line says which models recommend ranks, as `from: ● mine · ○ all · ○ ✓ selected`. The one in use has the filled dot `●` and is bold, as a tab that is on, with `(filtered)` while a search, a dropdown or a bound narrows them too. `F` and `E` narrow none, as a favorite already takes its own tier and an excluded model is never recommended. With `all` it ranks the models you have no access to too, each greyed as its row in the table is, and with `not available` under the grid. A `warning:` under the legend says that models without a score for the task aren't recommended for it, and that `d` on a tier can still pick one.<br><br>The cursor starts on the `from` line, and `k` from the tiers' header or `gg` goes back to it. There `h` `l` move over the entries with something to show, and `enter`, `space` or a click picks one. The panel stays open, and the table is on that tab after it. A tab's key leaves recommend for that tab, as a click on the tab does.<br><br>`j` `k` move between tasks, staying on the name or on the tier, and `h` `l` move along a task's row to a tier. `space` `o` `x` `H` `y` `f` `e` `i` act on the tier's model, and the table's cursor follows it when the table has it, so `R` again or `esc` lands on it (a model to download is in `a`). Where no model is under the cursor, as on a task's name, the tiers' header or `+ new task`, `space` does what `enter` does.<br><br>Each tier has a `▾` on its last line with room for it, as a header with a dropdown does, grey except on the tier under the cursor. `d` on a tier, or a click on its `▾`, opens its dropdown. `recommended:` comes first: the model the tier picks with no favorite. After it come all the models recommend ranks at the tier's price or under, best score first and unscored ones last, and the favorite, whatever the filters and the cap, with a `★` and the cursor on it. `/` searches. `space` or `enter` makes a model the tier's favorite, as `f` does, and `recommended` clears the favorite. Either then opens `H`'s list for the tier's model, its first line naming the harness it starts on by default, and `esc` skips it. On a task of your own the first entry is `none`.<br><br>`enter` on a model opens its details. On a task's name it shows the task's price frontier in the table: the best model at each price level, best first, each row cheaper and scoring lower. Your own tasks come last, each tier with the model you gave it, and `enter` on one's name goes to its cheapest model in the table. `+ new task` comes after them. `enter` or `space` there adds a task of your own: you type its name and a description under the grid, where a task's show, as with `T` (`Tool Dispatch` is saved as `tool-dispatch`). The description is needed, as agents know when to use the task by it. `esc` cancels, and a name a task already has, with nothing written about it, goes to that task. `d` on the new task's tiers picks its models. `T` on a task of your own edits it on its row (see `T`). `D` on its name deletes it, with its models and description. The first `D` asks, and a second `D` confirms |
| `t` | theme panel: `j` `k` preview a theme, `/` searches, `space` or `enter` saves, and `esc` or `t` closes. The themes are `terminal` (the terminal's own colours, the default), then the dark `gruvbox`, `nord`, `catppuccin`, `dracula`, `tokyonight`, `kanagawa`, `monokai`, `github`, `solarized`, `synthwave` and `cyberpunk`, the light `github-light`, `sepia` and `catppuccin-light`, and the retro `amber`, `phosphor`, `c64` and `gameboy`. Saved in `user.json`. A theme sets the 16 terminal colours, the text colour and 7 to 14 colours for developers, each of which has to read well on that theme's background and stay distinct from the others. Every theme paints its own background, so a dark one stays dark on a light terminal. Only `terminal` keeps a transparent background, except for a selected row's fill and the cursor's |

## Input and mouse

| Key | Action |
|-----|--------|
| typing | `←` `→` `^b` `^f` move, `^a` `^e` `Home` `End` go to the start / end, and `alt-b` `alt-f` `^←` `^→` `alt-←` `alt-→` move by word. Under a `/` search `↓` `↑` move the table's cursor, and in a panel or a list they go to the next / previous match. `^d` deletes the character under the cursor, `^w` `alt-backspace` `alt-d` delete a word, and `^u` `^k` delete to the start / end. A paste is typed as one line. When nothing is being typed, a paste only says there is nothing to paste into |
| mouse | click a row to highlight it. A double click opens what its cell shows: the details from the name or developer, a harness from Via (as `x` does), and from a number its page in the browser. For a price, the context or the release date that is the model's page on models.dev, else that of the provider you'd pay. For the other numbers it is the source that measures them: epoch.ai for ECI and Coding, arena.ai's agent leaderboard for Agentic, or the chat or WebDev one for a rating picked in a dropdown, and openrouter.ai for Tok/s. A double click on a note opens it for writing, as `i` does, even when it is empty. A click on the note being written, or on the status bar's prompt, keeps it, and a click anywhere else leaves it, as `esc` does. Any other empty cell opens nothing, and neither does a number on a site with no page for the model.<br><br>Ctrl click adds a row to the highlight or removes it, shift click or a drag across rows highlights a range, and a plain click drops the highlight. A right click selects the row. A click on a row's `✓` selects or deselects it, on its `☆` opens its tasks, and on its `✗` excludes it. A click on a tab shows it, and on `✓` `★` `✗` turns them on or off as `S` `F` `E` do. A click on the `#` header goes to the first row. A click on a header sorts by it, in compare too, and on its ▾ opens the dropdown. In the dropdown clicks toggle entries, and a click on an entry's `✗` leaves it out as `e` does, until a click elsewhere closes it. A click on a key hint in the status bar presses that key, when the hint names a single key. A click on the frame's `⚠` opens the issues, as `!` does.<br><br>In compare, recommend and `?` a click on a model or a key moves the cursor to it, and a double click on a key in `?` waits for its new key. In recommend a click on a tier moves to it, and a click elsewhere on a task's row moves to its name. A double click does what `enter` does there, and a right click in recommend does what `space` does. A click outside an open panel (keys, details, compare, recommend) closes it, as `esc` does.<br><br>The wheel scrolls, and the sideways wheel (or shift, alt or ctrl + wheel) moves the column, or the model in recommend. A click on a yellow `‹` `›` `▲` `▼` moves a screen that way: a page, as `PgUp` `PgDn` do, or a screen of columns, starting the view at the first column that was cut off (in compare, a screen further). So a phone or a mouse alone can get around: a vertical swipe scrolls and a tap on `‹` `›` moves sideways. With a prompt open, the click also leaves the prompt, as any click does |
